01 About Solviqo

We build the part off the shelf software never quite fits.

Every business has a way of working that makes it different. Most software makes you trade that away. We build the tools that let you keep it.

Web appsCustomer portalsInternal toolsWorkflow automationERP and CRM integrationsApproval systemsNew product MVPsLive dashboardsWeb appsCustomer portalsInternal toolsWorkflow automationERP and CRM integrationsApproval systemsNew product MVPsLive dashboards

Solviqo started from a pattern we kept seeing inside growing companies. The big systems could technically do almost anything. Getting them to do one specific, obviously useful thing took months, because that request competed with projects worth a hundred times more.

In the meantime, the real work kept happening anyway. In a spreadsheet five people had access to. In a chat thread that counted as an approval record. On paper, in a folder, in someone's head. The software existed. It just was not where the work happened.

So we built a studio around closing that gap. We are a small team based in India, working with businesses at home and around the world. We scope tightly, price before we start, build properly, and ship in weeks. Sometimes that is a new product, sometimes an internal tool, sometimes the missing link between two systems you already own.

03 How we operate

Four principles, held the same way on every project.

PRINCIPLE 01

Precise, not clever

Every scope is written down before we start: what gets built, what it does on day one, and when it ships. No adjectives standing in for a delivery date.

PRINCIPLE 02

Engineered, not experimental

Role based access, a real database, a documented integration. We build the way a systems integrator ships to spec, not the way a prototype gets taped together.

PRINCIPLE 03

Name the actual gap

If the problem is a six month queue or a process living in a chat thread, we say that plainly. Most of the pitch is naming what everyone already knows.

PRINCIPLE 04

Confident, not loud

No exclamation points, no claim of reinventing anything. A fixed price and a working app in weeks carries its own argument.
